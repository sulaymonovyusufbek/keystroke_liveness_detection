# Attackers (Phase 2)

_Run mode: **quick**, seed 42, Phase 1 config hash `107e859345c78e77` (frozen). Generated by `scripts/10_fit_attackers.py`; every number below is the value used by the generators._

## Threat model

Non-adaptive attackers with population-level typing statistics. Every attacker parameter is fitted ONLY on `attacker_pool` sessions (users disjoint from train / val / test, asserted in code). Attackers never query the detector and are never modified after detector results are seen. Each attacker types the SAME target sentence as the matched human session (one bot per human session per split; A5a is the exception, it replays a recording with its own sentence) and all bot output goes through the unchanged `features.py`. Keycode conventions are those of A1.

## Data used to fit the attackers

* attacker_pool: 200 users, 2,881 clean sessions.
* FIT users (parameters of A2-A5): 160 users, 2,310 sessions.
* HELD-OUT users: 40 users, 571 sessions (20% of pool users, seeded). Used only for the realism report (KS / mean difference), never for fitting or tuning.
* Statistics extracted: 80,412 char->char transitions (76,686 non-pause, 3,726 pauses), 1,964 session speed offsets, 3,256 Backspace runs, 3,257 Shift holds (147 overlapping).

Definitions used by all fitted statistics (computed on FIT sessions, rows sorted by PRESS_TIME, KEYSTROKE_ID as tie-break only):

* *character row*: LETTER is a single character consistent with KEYCODE (`char_to_key(LETTER)` gives KEYCODE); everything else (garbled LETTER, numpad, Delete, arrows) is a non-character row that interrupts adjacency. Shift rows (16) are skipped when looking at neighbouring keys.
* `pp` = PRESS_next − PRESS_this and `flight` = PRESS_next − RELEASE_this between consecutive non-Shift rows; a *pause* is `flight > 500` ms (the definition of the `pause_count` feature).
* digraph = (lower-cased previous character, lower-cased current character), i.e. two consecutive character rows with nothing but Shift in between.
* min_samples = 30: distributions with fewer samples are not used (backoff).

## Conventions shared by all generators

* Shift = keycode 16, LETTER `SHIFT`; Backspace = keycode 8, LETTER `BKSP`; letters use the uppercase ASCII keycode (`a`/`A` → 65), digits their ASCII code, space 32, punctuation the US-layout table of `kl/attacks/a1_gaussian.py`; unmapped characters get keycode 229.
* Times are integer ms; the first press of a bot session is 1,500,000,010,000 ms (arbitrary epoch origin); KEYSTROKE_ID = 1..n in press order.
* pp is clipped to [1, 5000] ms and holds to [1, 2000] ms (the limits of the Phase 1 cleaning rules) in A3/A4.
* RNG of bot *i* of attacker *a* on split *s*: `default_rng([seed, code(a), code(s), i])` (A1 1, A2 2, A3 3, A4 4, A5a 5, A5b 6; train 1, val 2, test 3, pool_heldout 4), so results do not depend on the number of workers.
* Shift handling of A2/A3/A4 (`common.place_shift`): Shift is pressed `lead` ms before the shifted key (never earlier than 1 ms after the previous key press) and, unless A4 draws an overlap, released `lag` ms after the key release but strictly before the next key press (no overlap). `lead`, `lag` (and `tail` for A4 overlaps) are drawn from the pool's empirical Shift statistics (tables under A4).

## A1 — independent Gaussians (Phase 1, unchanged)

hold = max(30, N(116.24, 23.88)) ms; flight = max(60, N(238.656, 111.6)) ms; PRESS_next = RELEASE_prev + flight; Shift nested around the key (Shift-down → flight → key → 30 ms → Shift-up). No fitted parameters.

## A2 — per-digraph empirical

Every pp is drawn independently from the empirical pp distribution of the digraph; every hold from the empirical hold distribution of the key. Backoff when a distribution has < 30 samples: digraph → second character → global (holds: key → global). Negative flights arise whenever hold > pp.

* digraphs with their own distribution: 303; second-character distributions: 33; per-key hold distributions: 35.
* the full tables are in `pool.joblib` (`PoolStats`) and the digraph table in `digraph_table.csv`.

## A3 — conditional / temporal

Per session: draw the speed offset `s` from the pool's per-session offsets, then

```
log pp_i = mu(c_{i-1}, c_i) + s + phi * (log pp_{i-1} - mu(c_{i-2}, c_{i-1}) - s) + eps_i,   eps_i ~ N(0, sigma^2)   (residual state starts at 0)
```

* `mu` = mean log pp of non-pause char->char transitions per digraph (backoff second char → global 4.9956); 296 digraph means.
* `s` of a pool session = mean over its non-pause transitions of (log pp − mu(digraph)), i.e. the session log-mean pp adjusted for its digraph composition (needs ≥ 10 transitions); mean over sessions 0.0192624.
* **phi = 0.00337394**, **sigma = 0.406524** (pooled least squares of the residual e_i = log pp_i − mu − s on e_{i-1} over 69,460 adjacent transition pairs; sigma = std of the fit residual).
* hold: per-key empirical draw × exp(b · (s − mean s)) with **b = 0.191726** (OLS slope of per-session mean log hold on s; intercept 4.59973).
* no pauses are generated by A3 (the mu / phi / sigma fit excludes flights above the pause threshold; A4 adds pauses).

## A4 — realistic imitator (A3 + behaviour)

All behavioural rates are drawn per session from empirical pool distributions.

* **Pauses**: for each char->char transition, with probability `p_boundary` (previous character is a space) or `p_within` (otherwise) the transition is replaced by a pause: PRESS = RELEASE_prev + flight, flight drawn from the pool's pause flights (flight > threshold); the AR state resets. `(c, u, p_boundary, p_within)` are drawn jointly from one random pool session; a rate is defined for a session with ≥ 3 transitions of its kind.
* **Errors**: substitution typos with adjacent US-QWERTY keys (letters only; key centres within 1.3 key widths with row stagger 0 / 0.25 / 0.75), case preserved. Number of corrected events = stochastic_round(c · L / mean_backspaces_per_event); number of uncorrected typos = stochastic_round(u · L) (L = sentence length), positions uniform among letters, non-overlapping windows. A corrected event = typo, d intended characters, d + 1 Backspaces, then the intended characters again; USER_INPUT = target with the uncorrected typos.
* **Correction delay d ∈ {0,1,2,3}**: from the pool's Backspace runs (run of b consecutive Backspace presses → d = b − 1). ASSUMPTION: runs longer than 4 presses (5.1% of runs) are word-level deletions and are excluded from this distribution; their Backspaces are still counted in `c`. P(d) = [0.5753, 0.2658, 0.1055, 0.0534]; mean Backspaces per event = 1.6371.
* **Timing after Backspace**: pp of char→Backspace, Backspace→Backspace, Backspace→char transitions and Backspace holds are drawn from the pool's empirical distributions (below); the AR state resets.
* **Shift overlap**: per session `p_so` = share of Shift holds released after the next key press (drawn from a random pool session with Shift holds); each shifted character overlaps with that probability, Shift being released `tail` ms after the next key press (pool distribution), otherwise as in A2/A3.
* **Rollover** comes from the A3 timing (hold independent of pp); it is checked against the pool in `realism_report.csv` (feature `rollover_rate`), not tuned.

## A5 — replay

* **A5a (verbatim)**: a random FIT-pool session is replayed exactly (own sentence, keystrokes and timings; the `sentence` argument is ignored). It is a real human recording; the detector is EXPECTED to accept it. It marks the boundary of session-level behavioural liveness (motivating challenge sentences and freshness checks), not a detector failure to fix.
* **A5b (transplanted)**: the hold / flight sequence of a random FIT-pool session (over all its rows in press order, Shift and Backspace rows included) is applied in order to the keystrokes of the target sentence (one Shift row before each shifted character, counted as a keystroke of the sequence). Target longer than the source → the sequence is continued by contiguous chunks (hold[j:], joined by flight[j−1]) of other random FIT-pool sessions; shorter → truncated. USER_INPUT = target.

## Assumptions and choices made where the specification is silent

1. `s` (A3) is the digraph-adjusted session log-mean pp, so that `mu + s` does not double count digraph effects.
2. A3/A4 timing is fitted on non-pause transitions; pauses are added only by A4 (otherwise they would be counted twice).
3. Word boundary = the previous character is a space.
4. Correction delay from Backspace-run lengths (see A4); Backspace rows are counted in `c` for all runs.
5. Typos exist only for letters a-z; digits, punctuation and non-ASCII characters are never mistyped.
6. A2/A3/A4 Shift rows never overlap another key press unless A4 draws an overlap; the pool's Shift lead/lag/tail are used instead of A1's fixed values.
7. Non-finite-feature bots (e.g. zero duration) are dropped and counted in `phase2/generation_report.json`.
8. Validation bots are not generated: thresholds of every detector use validation HUMANS only.
9. A5a is never a training attack for D3 (it is a real human recording); it is a test column only.
